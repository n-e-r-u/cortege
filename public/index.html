            In case of

            Counter-protesters or hostile groups
            • Stay in groups and avoid going off alone, especially before and after the protest.
            • Move to a safer area with marshals, medics, or a crowd of other protesters.
            • Report threats on the map to warn others. Don't post photos or names of individuals.
            • If injured, call 911. Contact your legal team or a trusted person for help.
            Orders to disperse
            • Police may order dispersal. Pay attention to clear verbal orders, announcements, or dispersal orders.
            • If ordered to disperse: leave calmly and by the clearest route. Avoid running toward police lines.
            • Laws vary by location - follow lawful orders to disperse. Refusing a lawful dispersal order can carry consequences in some jurisdictions.
            Police stops & questioning
            • You have the right to remain silent. You can say "I am going to remain silent" and ask for a lawyer.
            • Ask if you're free to leave. If yes, calmly walk away. If no, you are being detained.
            • ID requirements vary. In some states you must identify yourself if lawfully stopped and asked; in many others there is no general duty to show ID while just protesting. Know your local laws.
            • Do not argue, resist, or physically interfere. Stay calm and document if safe.
            Arrest
            • Stay silent. Give only your name if legally required in your situation, then say you want to remain silent and speak to a lawyer.
            • Ask for a lawyer immediately. You have the right to an attorney. If you can't afford one, you may be entitled to a public defender.
            • You have the right to make a local phone call. Let someone know you've been arrested if possible.
            • Do not consent to searches. Say "I do not consent to a search." Police may still search in some circumstances, but refusing consent is important.
            • Do not sign anything without talking to a lawyer.
            Your phone & privacy
            • Use a passcode, not biometrics. In some jurisdictions, police may compel fingerprint/face unlock more easily than a passcode.
            • Lock your screen. Turn off lock screen notifications showing sensitive content.
            • You do not have to unlock your phone for police without a warrant in many situations, but laws are evolving. Do not consent.
            • Consider backing up photos elsewhere. Phones can be seized.
            Filming police
            • You have the right to film police in public spaces while they're performing official duties.
            • Stay at a safe distance and don't interfere with police actions.
            • Police cannot order you to stop recording solely because they're performing public duties.
            • Be careful about sharing footage that could endanger others - blur faces when possible.
            Protesting in public
            • Public spaces: You generally have First Amendment rights to protest on sidewalks, in parks, and in public streets, though permits may be required for large marches or street closures.
            • Private property: You need permission to protest on private property. You can be asked to leave.
            • Counterprotesting: Counterprotesters also have First Amendment rights. Stay peaceful.
            • Time, place, and manner restrictions must be content-neutral and leave open alternative channels of communication.
            After an arrest
            • Document everything you remember as soon as it's safe to do so.
            • Get contact info for witnesses if possible.
            • Seek medical attention for injuries and document them with photos.
            • Contact a lawyer or legal support as soon as possible. Reach out to your legal team if available.
            • Consider contacting the ACLU or local legal aid organizations for support.
            • Poison control: If someone needs poisoning help, call 1-800-222-1222 (U.S. Poison Control).

            General information about U.S. constitutional rights, not legal advice. Laws vary by state and locality. If arrested or detained, speak to a qualified attorney about your specific situation. In an emergency, call 911.
